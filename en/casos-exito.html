Internal cases

Before we ask you to trust us,
we run it inside our own company.

Every piece we offer runs a real part of D-Code Partners first. These are three real cases — including what worked and what did not go the way we expected.

Inside D-Code

How we apply our own systems.

01 Sales

From detected company to proposal sent, without anyone having to remember to do it

ProblemSpotting sales opportunities by sector and turning them into proposals depended on someone manually reviewing a list every week — with the risk that it simply wouldn't get done.
BeforeThe opportunity radar existed, but moving from "interesting company" to "proposal sent" was a manual step with no fixed deadline.
AutomationEvery Monday, the system automatically converts the highest-scoring companies into leads and triggers an AI-generated proposal — which goes through human review before it goes out.
What We LearnedAutomating detection and the proposal doesn't close the sale by itself — it creates the opportunity for a conversation. It still takes a person to turn that into an actual call.
02 Operations

Distributing the team's workload without anyone having to decide it every hour

ProblemWhenever we closed a deal, creating the project file, assigning tasks, and tracking deadlines depended on someone remembering to do it amid the rest of the day's work.
BeforeCreating projects and assigning tasks were manual steps, at the mercy of the workload of whoever had to handle them that day.
AutomationWhen a project closes, the system creates it on its own; every hour it distributes pending tasks based on each person's actual workload; every day it flags what's about to come due.
What We LearnedIt's the system we use most internally today, precisely because it automates task distribution and deadlines — the kind of small, repetitive decision that eats up more time than it looks like.
03 Internal discipline, not automation

When our hypothesis about a failure didn't match what the data actually said

ProblemOur own internal audit system had been flagging one specific workflow as the likely cause of an elevated error rate across our automations.
BeforeWe nearly stepped in and modified that workflow based on that hypothesis, without first checking it against the actual runs.
AutomationInstead of acting on the hypothesis, we audited the real executions one by one — and the actual concentration of errors was in two different workflows than the ones flagged.
What We LearnedThis isn't a case about automation — it's a case about discipline: we don't fix a failure until we've verified its cause against direct evidence, even when the suspicion comes from our own system.
