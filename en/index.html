02 — The problem

07 — Cases

Before we ask you to trust us, we run it inside our own company.

Three real D-Code Partners cases — including what worked and what did not go the way we expected.

Sales From detected company to proposal sent, without anyone having to remember to do it

  1. Before

    The opportunity radar existed, but moving from "interesting company" to "proposal sent" was a manual step with no fixed deadline.

  2. Problem

    Turning sector opportunities into proposals depended on someone manually reviewing a list every week.

  3. System

    Every Monday, the system converts the highest-scoring companies into leads and triggers an AI proposal — which goes through human review before it goes out.

  4. After

    Automating detection and the proposal doesn't close the sale by itself — it creates the opportunity for a conversation. It still takes a person.

Delivery Distributing the team's workload without anyone having to decide it every hour

  1. Before

    Creating projects and assigning tasks were manual steps, at the mercy of the workload of whoever had to handle them that day.

  2. Problem

    Creating the project file, assigning tasks and tracking deadlines depended on someone remembering amid the rest of the day's work.

  3. System

    When a project closes, the system creates it; every hour it distributes tasks by each person's actual workload; every day it flags what's about to come due.

  4. After

    It's the system we use most internally today: it automates the small, repetitive decisions that eat up more time than they look like.

Internal discipline When our hypothesis about a failure didn't match what the data actually said

  1. Before

    We nearly modified a workflow based on a hypothesis, without first checking it against the actual runs.

  2. Problem

    Our internal audit system had been flagging that workflow as the likely cause of an elevated error rate.

  3. System

    We audited the real executions one by one — and the errors were concentrated in two different workflows than the ones flagged.

  4. After

    We don't fix a failure until we've verified its cause against direct evidence, even when the suspicion comes from our own system.
